\section*{Executive summary}
This paper analyzes the probability $P(R,T)$ that a spherical region of radius $R$ in a homogeneous free Fermi gas is completely empty at finite temperature. The central question is how this \emph{hole probability} crosses over from classical Poisson-like behavior at high temperature to strongly correlated Pauli-driven behavior at low temperature. The main result is a sharp low-temperature large-radius scaling law
\[
P(R,T)\sim \exp\big(-(k_F R)^{d+1}\Phi_d(u)\big),
\qquad u=\frac{2RT}{k_F}=\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{R}{\xi_T},
\]
where $k_F$ is the Fermi momentum and $\xi_T$ the thermal correlation length. The striking point is that the scaling function $\Phi_d(u)$ has a genuine nonanalytic transition at the universal critical value
\[
u_c=\frac{2}{\pi},
\]
of order $\frac32(d+1)$. In the Coulomb-gas reformulation, this transition corresponds to the opening of a macroscopic gap in the optimal momentum-space density that realizes the rare event.

So this is not just a crossover story. In the joint limit $k_F R\to\infty$, $T/T_F\to0$ with $u$ fixed, the rare-fluctuation mechanism itself changes phase.

\section*{Physical setting and observable}
The model is the grand-canonical free Fermi gas in $d$ dimensions at temperature $T=1/\beta$ and chemical potential $\tilde\mu$, with fixed average density $\rho$. The one-body occupation factor is
\[
\sigma(k)=\frac{1}{e^{\beta(k^2/2-\tilde\mu)}+1},
\]
and the free fermions form a determinantal point process. The observable is the hole probability
\[
P(R,T)=\mathbb P(\mathcal N_R=0)=\lim_{s\to\infty}\langle e^{-s\mathcal N_R}\rangle,
\]
where $\mathcal N_R$ counts particles in the ball of radius $R$.

At very high temperature one expects classical independent-particle behavior, so for a Poisson process one has
\[
P^{\rm Poi}(R)=e^{-\rho V_d R^d}.
\]
At zero temperature, the Pauli principle makes emptiness much rarer, and the known asymptotic is
\[
P(R,0)\sim e^{-\kappa_d (k_F R)^{d+1}},
\qquad
\kappa_d=\frac{2}{(d+1)^2\Gamma(d+1)}.
\]
The paper asks: what happens between these two regimes, and can the crossover be described sharply in any dimension?

\section*{Main asymptotic regimes}
The paper identifies two different large-$R$ regimes.

\subsection*{1. High-temperature / classical regime}
For fixed $T\sim T_F$ and $R/\lambda_T\gg1$, with $\lambda_T$ the thermal de Broglie wavelength,
\[
\log P(R,T)\simeq -(R\sqrt T)^d A_d(T/T_F) + (R\sqrt T)^{d-1} B_d(T/T_F).
\]
The functions $A_d$ and $B_d$ are given explicitly in terms of polylogarithms:
\[
A_d(t)= -\frac{1}{2^{d/2}\Gamma(1+d/2)}\,\mathrm{Li}_{d/2+1}(-e^{\beta\tilde\mu}),
\]
\[
B_d(t)= \frac{2^{(d-3)/2}}{\pi\Gamma(d)}\int_0^{\infty} dv\, v^{(d-1)/2}
\big[\mathrm{Li}_{1/2}(-e^{\beta\tilde\mu}-v)\big]^2.
\]
In the extreme high-temperature limit $T/T_F\gg1$, $A_d$ reduces so that the Poisson law is recovered. So the leading term is volume-like and classical, exactly as expected.

\subsection*{2. Low-temperature / quantum large-deviation regime}
For $T\ll T_F$ and $k_F R\gg1$ with
\[
u = \frac{2RT}{k_F}=\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{R}{\xi_T}
\]
held fixed, the hole probability obeys
\[
\log P(R,T)\simeq -(k_F R)^{d+1}\Phi_d(u).
\]
This is the main scaling regime. The authors compute $\Phi_d(u)$ exactly in any dimension and show that it has two branches,
\[
\Phi_d(u)=
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{d,+}(u), & u>2/\pi,\\
\Phi_{d,-}(u), & u<2/\pi.
\end{cases}
\]
The two branches meet nonanalytically at $u_c=2/\pi$, with
\[
\Phi_{d,+}(u)-\Phi_{d,-}(u)\propto (u_c-u)^{\frac32(d+1)}
\qquad (u\uparrow u_c).
\]
Hence the transition is of order $\frac32(d+1)$.

\section*{Explicit scaling functions}
For $u>2/\pi$, the function $\Phi_{d,+}(u)$ is a polynomial in $1/u$ of degree $d+1$. The paper gives explicit formulas, e.g.
\[
\Phi_{1,+}(u)=\frac{4}{3\pi u}-\frac{1}{\pi^2 u^2},
\]
\[
\Phi_{2,+}(u)=\frac{1}{8u}-\frac{8}{15\pi^2u^2}+\frac{1}{12\pi^2u^3},
\]
\[
\Phi_{3,+}(u)=\frac{4}{45\pi u}-\frac{1}{6\pi^2u^2}+\frac{16}{105\pi^3u^3}-\frac{1}{18\pi^4u^4}.
\]
These large-$u$ forms match the asymptotics from the high-temperature/Widom regime, so the two asymptotic theories glue consistently.

For $u<2/\pi$, the zero-temperature-like branch depends on parity of $d$. Examples given in the paper are
\[
\Phi_{1,-}(u)=\frac12-\frac{\pi^2u^2}{48},
\qquad
\Phi_{3,-}(u)=\frac{1}{48}-\frac{\pi^2u^2}{1440}+\frac{\pi^4u^4}{53760},
\]
and in $d=2$,
\[
\Phi_{2,-}(u)=
\sqrt{4-\pi^2u^2}\left(-\frac{\pi^2u^2}{1440}+\frac{9}{40\pi^2u^2}+\frac{7}{360}\right)
-\frac{8}{15\pi^2u^2}
+\left(\frac{1}{6\pi^3u^3}+\frac{1}{4\pi u}\right)\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{\pi u}{2}\right).
\]
At $u=0$, one recovers exactly the known zero-temperature coefficient $\kappa_d$, as one should.

\section*{What the transition means}
The really nice part is the mechanism. The hole probability is mapped to an effective Coulomb gas in momentum space. In that picture, the rare event ``a large real-space hole is empty'' is realized by an optimal momentum density $\rho^*(p)$ minimizing an energy functional. The support of that optimizer changes qualitatively at $u_c=2/\pi$.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item For $u>u_c$, the optimal density reaches down to zero momentum: no macroscopic momentum gap.
\item For $u<u_c$, the support is shifted away from the origin: a genuine momentum gap opens.
\end{itemize}

So the nonanalyticity of $\Phi_d(u)$ is not an accident of formulas; it reflects a change in the constrained optimal configuration of the Coulomb gas. The paper interprets this as a sharp change in the mechanism of rare fluctuations.

A good intuition is: when thermal effects are still strong enough compared with the region size ($u>u_c$), the optimal rare event uses a ``soft'' reorganization of occupations. Below the threshold ($u<u_c$), emptiness is so demanding that the system creates a hard depletion window in momentum space.

\section*{Determinantal and angular decomposition}
The exact starting point is the Fredholm determinant formula
\[
P(R,T)=\det(I-\Pi_R K),
\]
where $K$ is the finite-temperature fermionic kernel. In dimensions $d>1$, the authors use angular decomposition to factor the problem into independent radial sectors labeled by angular momentum $\ell$:
\[
P(R,T)=\prod_{\ell\ge0} \big(P^{(\ell)}(R,T)\big)^{g_d(\ell)},
\]
where $g_d(\ell)$ is the degeneracy and each sector is expressed as a Fredholm determinant involving a finite-temperature Bessel kernel,
\[
P^{(\ell)}(R,T)=\det(I-\Pi_{[0,R]} \hat K_\nu),
\qquad \nu=\ell+\frac d2-1.
\]
This reduction is important because it turns the $d$-dimensional problem into a family of effectively one-dimensional problems, which can then be attacked by random-matrix/Coulomb-gas tools.

\section*{Coulomb-gas reformulation}
A duality relation rewrites each sector's Fredholm determinant as a linear statistic for the zero-temperature Bessel determinantal process. From there, the event is represented by minimizing a Coulomb-gas energy
\[
E_\nu[\rho]
= -\frac12\iint \log|p-p'|\,\hat\rho(p)\hat\rho(p')\,dp\,dp'
+ \frac12\int \rho(p)\log\!\left(1+e^{-\beta(p/(2R^2)-\tilde\mu)}\right)dp,
\]
with positivity constraints and a background equilibrium density. In the large-$R$ limit,
\[
P^{(\ell)}(R,T)\sim e^{-2E_{0,\nu}},
\qquad E_{0,\nu}=\min_{\rho\ge0} E_\nu[\rho].
\]
The minimizer develops the support transition mentioned above.

For fixed angular sector $\ell=O(1)$, the paper already finds a transition at $u_c=2/\pi$, with an asymptotic of the form
\[
\log P^{(\ell)}(R,T)\simeq -z^2
\begin{cases}
\frac{2}{3\pi u}-\frac{1}{2\pi^2u^2}, & u>2/\pi,\\
\frac14-\frac{\pi^2u^2}{96}, & u<2/\pi,
\end{cases}
\qquad z=k_FR.
\]
But for the full $d$-dimensional hole probability, the dominant contribution comes from large angular momentum sectors $\ell\sim R$, not finite $\ell$. The authors therefore solve the Coulomb-gas problem in the scaling $\ell\sim \nu\sim \lambda z$ and integrate over $\lambda$.

That yields the exact representation
\[
-\log P(R,T)\simeq \frac{2}{\Gamma(d-1)} z^{d+1}
\int_0^1 d\lambda\, \lambda^{d-2} \phi\big(\lambda,\tilde a(u,\lambda)\big),
\]
which becomes the explicit scaling function $\Phi_d(u)$ after a change of variables. The lower limit of the integral is controlled by the critical support edge $\tilde a_c(u)$, and this is exactly where the transition enters.

\section*{Critical region}
The paper also identifies a critical scaling window around the transition. If
\[
y=(2k_FR)^{2/3}\frac{u-u_c}{u_c},
\]
then in the regime $y=O(1)$,
\[
P(R,T)\simeq G_d(y)\, e^{-(k_FR)^{d+1}\Phi_{d,+}(u)}.
\]
The scaling function $G_d(y)$ tends to $1$ for $y\to+\infty$, while for $y\to-\infty$ it reproduces the singular matching needed for the two branches. The temperature scale of the critical window is
\[
\delta T \sim T_c (k_F R)^{-2/3}
\sim T_F (k_F R)^{-5/3},
\qquad T_c=\frac{k_F}{\pi R}.
\]
So the transition sharpens with increasing $R$, and the critical region becomes parametrically narrow.

\section*{Why the result matters}
There are several reasons this is a strong paper.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item It gives an exact large-deviation scaling function for a nontrivial finite-temperature fermionic observable in any dimension.
\item It identifies a universal critical value $u_c=2/\pi$ and a clear order of nonanalyticity.
\item It ties together free-fermion determinantal structure, Widom asymptotics, Bessel/sine kernels, and Coulomb-gas variational methods.
\item It is experimentally relevant: the hole probability is directly measurable in quantum gas microscope setups.
\end{itemize}

Conceptually, the nicest point is that even for \emph{free} fermions, a sufficiently sharp rare-event question reveals a bona fide phase transition in the optimal fluctuation problem.

\section*{Numerics and checks}
The authors back the formulas with precise numerical evaluations of the relevant Fredholm determinants using Bornemann-type methods and finite-$z$ extrapolation. They also recover known rigorous results in $d=1$ and known $T=0$ formulas in any $d$, which makes the whole asymptotic picture feel very solid.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The most reusable structural idea is the \textbf{rare-event-as-Coulomb-gas-optimum} viewpoint: the nonanalyticity comes from a change in support of the optimizer, not from any interaction in the underlying fermion Hamiltonian.
\item The scaling variable
\[
u=\frac{2RT}{k_F}=\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{R}{\xi_T}
\]
is the right knob: it compares the hole size directly with the thermal correlation length.
\item For asymptotic matching problems, this paper is a nice example of combining \textbf{Widom/high-$T$ asymptotics} with a \textbf{low-$T$ Coulomb-gas large-deviation theory} and showing they meet consistently.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Bottom line}
A very elegant paper. It turns a natural full-counting-statistics observable for free fermions into an exactly analyzable variational problem and finds a universal finite-temperature transition in the hole-probability scaling function. If you're interested in determinantal processes, Coulomb gases, quantum large deviations, or the geometry of rare events, this one is absolutely worth reading.
